Joins are the quiet connective tissue of relational work. They let you answer questions that span more than one table without flattening your data model.

INNER JOIN

An inner join returns rows where the join condition matches on both sides.

SELECT o.order_id, c.name
FROM orders AS o
INNER JOIN customers AS c ON c.id = o.customer_id;

LEFT JOIN

A left join keeps every row from the left table. When there is no matching right-side row, the right-side columns are NULL.

SELECT c.name, o.order_id
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.id;

Key takeaways

  • Choose the preserved side deliberately.
  • Check key uniqueness before joining.
  • Filter joined data with care: a WHERE condition can remove the NULL rows you intended to preserve.
Interview tip: Explain both the result shape and the business meaning of the join. That is usually more valuable than reciting syntax.
